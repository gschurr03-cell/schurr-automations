Examples we can scope

Know what starts it. Know when it is done.

These are possible dealership workflows, not claims about live client installations. Supported connections and permissions determine what can be built.

Inquiry to assigned task.

A vehicle inquiry arrives; the system creates a task for the agreed salesperson. That person reviews the request and records the callback outcome. Completion is a saved outcome, not merely a sent alert.

Vehicle question to staff review.

A buyer asks about a vehicle, price or trade. Their question and supplied details go to authorized staff for review. Staff confirms the answer and next action; final prices and trade values stay with authorized people.

Follow-up records to a useful report.

At the agreed reporting time, available callback and appointment records are assembled. Your staff checks exceptions and approves the report. Requests, confirmed visits and unresolved gaps remain distinct.

The practical details

Before we build.

What is included in the scope?

A workflow quote specifies the source, destination, permitted actions, approval rules, exception handling, reporting, and maintenance. New departments, systems, data categories, or campaigns are separate changes.

Do we have to replace our CRM?

We inspect your current setup first. If it supports the workflow, we build around it. Any new software or paid connection is agreed before work begins.

Can staff approve important actions?

Yes. Price answers, buyer messages, and other decisions can stay behind a staff review step. We define who may approve, what happens after a rejection, and how stalled tasks are surfaced.

How do we measure the result?

We agree a starting point and an observable outcome: for example, completed buyer callbacks, staff questions resolved, or reports delivered with exceptions visible. A created task alone does not prove the work is finished.
